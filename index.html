Point a USB or Bluetooth scanner at the QR or barcode on the base or box label, or take a photo of it. HP, Dell, Lenovo, Fujitsu, Apple, Samsung, Microsoft and others are recognised, and each device lands here as a row with make, serial and part number pulled out.

How scanning works
  • Hardware scanner (keyboard wedge, USB or Bluetooth): the badge at the top must say Ready to scan. If it says paused, tap the scan box; inside the Claude app, keystrokes go to the chat box until you click the page. Scanners that end with Enter, Tab or nothing at all are all accepted. Multi-line QR payloads are captured whole, so labels that encode several fields on separate lines still land as one row.
  • Phone or webcam: Live scan opens the camera and adds each new code it sees, with a buzz and a green frame. It appears in the downloaded page when opened over https or from the PC's own disk; Claude's viewer does not allow live camera use, so inside Claude use Photo of label (several photos can be picked at once).
  • Understood formats: vendor support URLs (HP ?serialnumber=, Dell qrl.dell.com/<tag>, Lenovo pcsupport), Lenovo 1S<MTM><serial> codes, ANSI/ISO box labels (S serial, 1P part), GS1 (21) serials, labelled text (S/N, Service Tag, MTM, P/N, VFY:, IMEI, MAC), Apple part numbers (…/A), Samsung model codes, and HP's comma-separated QR (serial,product,model,1y1y0y, where the last part is years of parts/labour/on-site warranty) and HP box QR links (hpsmart.com/QRC/…/21/<serial>?240=<part>).
  • Phone barcode apps: apps that type into the PC (keyboard mode, e.g. Barcode to PC) work like a USB scanner. For apps that keep a scan history, export or share it as .csv or .txt and use Import list. Import list also reloads a list exported from this page (.xlsx or .csv), adding new devices and filling gaps in existing ones, or paste the list onto the page: each line becomes its own device.
  • Etched codes (laser-marked Data Matrix or QR on metal or dark plastic): a photo gets a slower second attempt with contrast boosted, which often works for etched QR codes. Etched Data Matrix codes on brushed metal are rarely readable from a photo; use a USB or Bluetooth 2D scanner for those.
  • Labels with several barcodes (S/N, P/N, MAC): a photo reads them all at once. Scanned one by one, start each device with its serial; the P/N, MAC and locale barcodes that follow join that row for two minutes. Other barcodes on labels and boxes (tracking, Box ID, SO, PO, shipment, UPC) are ignored, and HP spare/SSA numbers go to Notes. Scanning another label for a device already in the list fills its empty fields. The retail UPC/EAN box barcode is ignored because it does not identify the device. Make is detected from the URL, label text or serial pattern; a lone 7-character code is taken as a Dell service tag. Raw text is kept in the export.
  • Duplicate serials are not added again; the existing row is highlighted instead.
  • Built (est.) is filled for HP serials only, decoded from characters 4–6 (year digit + week), and is an estimate. Fill model names asks Claude to name the model from the make and part number; those values are marked AI and should be checked before they go into an asset register.
  • Rows save to your account when signed in, so you can scan on a phone and export on a laptop.
